**White Paper: Progressive Visual Exchange as a Conversational Primitive**  **Version 0.2.2 — 16 October 2026**
**Abstract**  Current digital communication treats images primarily as static artifacts—objects to be captioned, commented on, or regenerated in isolation. This paper introduces *progressive visual exchange* (PVE): a mode of interaction in which successive representations function as conversational turns that advance, modify, or respond to a shared state rather than restating it. Images serve as the immediate, practical implementation and testbed. The underlying protocol is intended to be more general—an early exploration of progressive exchange of perceptual representations. The approach is immediately testable with existing generative tools and supplies a design grammar that may remain relevant for future higher-bandwidth or non-linguistic channels, including potential brain-computer or sensory interfaces, without depending on their existence.
**1. Problem Statement**  Text remains the dominant channel for precise, revisable communication because it is sequential, editable, and relatively low-bandwidth. Images convey dense spatial, textural, and affective information in a single frame, yet current interfaces rarely exploit this density for dialogue. Typical image replies either (a) describe the original in text, (b) generate a thematically similar but disconnected image, or (c) apply superficial edits that do not constitute a response. In all three cases the second image does not inherit and modify the first as a shared scene. The result is a series of isolated statements rather than a continuing exchange.
**2. Concept: Progressive Visual Exchange**  A progressive visual exchange treats successive images as conversational states within an evolving interaction. Each image preserves relevant context from prior turns while introducing a meaningful response or change. In the initial implementation, this context is represented through a shared visual scene; broader implementations may use other perceptual representations or structured intermediate states. Upon receiving an image, the responding party generates a new image that:
- preserves recognizable continuity with the prior frame (setting, key objects, participants, lighting conditions);- introduces a clear next action, change of viewpoint, addition, or removal that addresses the content of the previous image;- remains interpretable as a reply rather than a parallel statement.
The mechanism is analogous to spoken turn-taking. Just as a verbal reply presupposes and advances the prior utterance, a progressive image presupposes and advances the prior visual state. Continuity constraints can be relaxed deliberately (scene change, time skip, metaphorical shift) when the conversational goal requires it, but the default is forward motion within an established context.
Images are the first practical modality. The same interaction pattern can, in principle, apply to other perceptual representations.
**Selective progressive exchange versus full state transfer.**  A progressive exchange does not attempt to transmit or receive the entire cognitive state of either participant. It transmits only those elements the sender elects to externalize in the current turn, under continuity constraints that make the contribution interpretable as a response rather than an independent statement. The receiving system (human or artificial) is not required to reconstruct the sender’s full knowledge; it is required only to preserve and advance the shared representational context that has been explicitly exchanged. This selectivity is both a safety property and a practical bandwidth property: the channel remains usable even if it is incomplete, noisy, or limited to particular modalities.
**3. Immediate Utility**  The pattern is implementable today with text-to-image and image-to-image models. It requires no new hardware. Preliminary informal observations suggest that users can distinguish acknowledgment-style generations from progressive ones and may prefer the latter for sustained exchange. Controlled evaluation is needed to establish the reliability and extent of this effect. Benefits include:
- higher information density per turn than text alone;- reduced need for lengthy verbal descriptions of spatial or emotional context;- a lightweight shared state that both parties can reference without re-uploading prior images.
**4. Long-Term Motivation: Beyond Language-Based Interaction**  Progressive Visual Exchange is designed for immediate experimentation with existing generative tools, but its broader motivation is to explore interaction protocols that could remain useful as communication technologies evolve.
If future brain-computer interfaces or other technologies enable increasingly direct exchange of sensory information, imagery, or perceptual representations, conventional text- and voice-centred conversational interfaces may become a limiting abstraction. In such a setting, an LLM (or other AI system) would need an interface capable of accepting and producing sensory or imagery-based messages rather than relying solely on language tokens. Such an interface would require not only sensory input and output channels, but also representations and processing mechanisms capable of interpreting, transforming, and communicating the relevant information with measurable fidelity.
The image-based implementation is a constrained testbed for a more general pattern: progressive exchange of perceptual or intentional representations between a biological nervous system and an artificial system. The target interaction is not unrestricted knowledge transfer or a full “mind meld.” It is a high-bandwidth, selective channel in which participants exchange successive, context-preserving representations of sensory content, spatial structure, or directed intent, while retaining the ability to accept, reject, modify, or redirect what is received.
In this framing, “telepathy” refers only to the reduction or elimination of intermediate symbolic or motor stages (typing, speech, visual display) in favor of more direct mapping between neural activity and an artificial system’s internal representations. It does not require complete mutual access to memories, beliefs, or unfiltered internal states. The protocol constraints remain those already stated: representational fidelity of the relevant content, continuity across turns, intentional progression, and correctability. These constraints are what distinguish a usable exchange from raw data dumping or uncontrolled leakage.
Images currently serve as a practical proxy because they are dense, human-interpretable perceptual representations that existing models can both generate and condition upon. The same interaction grammar—preserve relevant context, introduce a responsive change, allow correction—could apply to other substrates (neural latents, reconstructed sensory streams, structured intermediate states) if and when interfaces capable of exchanging them become available. The protocol itself does not depend on any particular neural technology existing; it only requires that whatever channel is used can support successive, revisable representations rather than one-shot transmission.
PVE explores an alternative interaction pattern in which participants exchange successive representations that preserve relevant context while advancing a shared interaction. The initial implementation uses generated images as a practical testbed. The underlying principles may eventually extend to other sensory modalities and representations, although their applicability to future neural or direct perceptual communication systems remains speculative.
PVE does not assume that telepathy, unrestricted thought transmission, or any particular future interface is possible or imminent. Its objective is to develop and evaluate useful interaction principles today that might also apply to communication methods not yet available. The protocol itself is independent of that assumption.
Core requirements for any such interface include:
- **Representational fidelity** — preserve relevant properties of the incoming experience or representation;- **Continuity** — maintain relevant information across successive exchanges;- **Intentional progression** — make the next contribution responsive to the preceding one;- **Correctability** — allow participants to accept, reject, modify, or redirect the developing representation.
**5. Points of Interest for Further Development**  The following areas are open for concrete work:
- **Continuity metrics.** Define measurable criteria (object persistence, viewpoint distance, lighting consistency, participant identity) that distinguish progressive replies from unrelated generations. Human preference studies can validate the metrics.- **Edit versus regenerate.** Compare full-image regeneration against localized editing or inpainting when producing a next state. Measure latency, coherence, and user control.- **Shared representations and state fidelity.** Investigate whether both parties can operate on a common intermediate representation (scene graph, neural latent, explicit 3D proxy, or other suitable perceptual representation) rather than exchanging only final pixels. Evaluate whether this improves consistency, reduces redundant computation, and preserves relevant information across successive turns.- **Turn signaling.** Establish lightweight conventions for indicating the intended type of move (continuation, contrast, zoom, time advance, metaphorical shift) without requiring accompanying text.- **Multi-party and asynchronous use.** Extend the pattern beyond two-person exchanges. Determine how branching, merging, or delayed replies affect coherence.- **Evaluation protocols.** Design repeatable tasks in which participants must achieve a joint goal (complete a story, resolve a spatial arrangement, convey a specific affect) using only progressive images. Compare success rate and subjective clarity against text-only and static-image baselines.- **Interface affordances.** Prototype simple controls that let a user accept, reject, or partially adopt elements of a received progressive image before generating their own reply.- **Safety and consent constraints.** Identify failure modes in which progressive generation produces unintended or harmful content, and define technical or policy mitigations appropriate to both current tools and future interfaces.
**6. Limitations and Risks**  Progressive exchange increases the risk of cumulative drift; small inconsistencies can compound across turns. It also raises authorship and consent questions when one party substantially alters elements introduced by the other. Computational cost is higher than text generation, particularly if high-resolution or multi-view consistency is required. These issues are manageable with explicit constraints and user controls but must be addressed rather than ignored.
**7. Conclusion**  Progressive visual exchange converts images from isolated artifacts into conversational turns. The technique is usable with present generative models and supplies a transferable interaction model. Systematic exploration of continuity rules, shared representations, evaluation methods, and interface controls can be undertaken immediately. The resulting design knowledge is intended to remain applicable whether the eventual medium is improved image generation, multi-sensory rendering, or other non-linguistic channels—should such channels become practical.
